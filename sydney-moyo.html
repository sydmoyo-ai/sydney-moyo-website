Sydney Moyo

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Biological Sciences at Louisiana State University. My work explores the connections between ecosystems, particularly how energy, nutrients, and contaminants move through freshwater, coastal, and terrestrial food webs.

My academic journey

My background is in zoology, and my research interests have developed around a central question: how are ecosystems connected, and what happens when those connections change? Over time, this has led me to study freshwater invertebrates, aquatic food webs, and the movement of resources and contaminants across ecosystem boundaries.

I completed my Ph.D. in 2016 and subsequently worked as a postdoctoral researcher with Michael Polito, expanding my work in food-web ecology and biogeochemistry. I joined Louisiana State University in spring 2024, where I am developing a research program spanning freshwater, wetland, coastal, and terrestrial ecosystems.

How I approach research

I combine field observations and experiments with stable isotope analysis, fatty-acid biomarkers, contaminant measurements, and ecological modeling. These approaches allow me to investigate both the structure of food webs and the processes that connect organisms and habitats.

My work includes research on aquatic insect emergence and terrestrial predators, food webs in southern African wetlands and rivers, coastal marsh restoration in Louisiana, freshwater biodiversity, and contaminant movement across aquatic–terrestrial boundaries.

Teaching and mentoring

Teaching and mentoring are important parts of my work. At LSU, I teach introductory biology and ecology, and I work with undergraduate and graduate researchers on projects that bring together field ecology, laboratory analysis, and quantitative approaches.

I aim to help students develop the skills to ask clear ecological questions, evaluate evidence critically, and communicate their findings with confidence.
